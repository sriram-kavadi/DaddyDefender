    What it checks

    In a link

    • No HTTPS
    • An IP address in place of a domain name
    • A username section before the real host (the @ trick)
    • Look-alike characters from other alphabets
    • Link shorteners that hide the destination
    • Many subdomains or several hyphens
    • Domains that imitate known brands through typos, swapped characters, or added words
    • Domain endings common in scam campaigns
    • Words such as login or verify in the address
    • Unusual ports, very long addresses, another address hidden in a parameter, and downloads that can run software

    In a message

    • Pressure to act quickly
    • Threats about locked accounts or legal trouble
    • Requests for passwords, one-time codes, card or ID numbers
    • Prizes, refunds, and rewards you did not ask for
    • Gift cards, crypto, wire transfers, and upfront fees
    • Generic greetings
    • Instructions to click a link or open an attachment
    • Requests for secrecy or remote access to your device
    • Claims to be a tax office, police, or courier
    • A brand name in the text with a link that goes somewhere else
    • Every link inside the message is analyzed on its own

    How the level is decided

    Each red flag adds points. The total is capped at 100 and mapped to a level. For a message, the points from the wording are added to the points of its riskiest link. A level is an estimate, so a low rating never proves something is safe.

    LevelScoreWhat it means
    Low0 to 24No strong warning signs found.
    Medium25 to 49Some warning signs. Verify through an official channel.
    High50 to 74Several signs that match common scam patterns.
    Critical75 to 100Strong match for phishing. Do not interact.

    What it cannot see

    This tool only reads the text you paste. It cannot see how old a domain is, whether a site has a valid certificate, where a link redirects, or whether an address is on a blocklist. Those checks need a server, and this site never sends your input anywhere. Treat the result as a second opinion, and when in doubt, contact the organization using details you already trust.
